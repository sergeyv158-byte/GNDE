Technology

Magnetostrictive guided-wave transduction

GNDE develops magnetostrictive transducers (MsT) that use static magnetic bias and time-varying magnetic fields to produce mechanical strain in ferromagnetic materials. Depending on the field orientation and sensor geometry, these interactions excite torsional modes in cylindrical structures or shear-horizontal guided waves in plates.

01 / FIELD ORIENTATION

Wiedemann effects

Conventional and reversed Wiedemann configurations employ different orientations of static and alternating magnetic fields to generate dynamic shear strain.

02 / FeCo TRANSDUCTION

Efficient wave excitation

Optimized biasing, coil winding and FeCo strips concentrate useful magnetic excitation in compact sensor geometries.

03 / ADAPTABLE FORM

Industrial-scale coverage

Compact strips, flexible bracelet arrays and sensor networks support inaccessible geometries, large structures and long-term monitoring.

High-efficiency excitation

Double-sided coils amplify magnetostrictive strain

Closely wound coils and optimized magnetic bias strengthen the effective alternating field in the active FeCo material. In the published comparison, two-sided excitation generated approximately 3.2–4.6× higher RMS strain than one-sided excitation between 30 and 120 kHz.

Greater strain can increase guided-wave signal amplitude, subject to transducer coupling, frequency, structure and geometry.

LOW-PROFILE DESIGN

As little as 2–3 mm overall height

Suitable designs use 0.5-inch-wide FeCo strips with a complete vertical profile as low as 2–3 mm, including permanent magnets. This enables sensing at restricted sites, including tank-bottom extensions with only about 0.5 inch of accessible lip.

FLEXIBLE ARRAYS

Nearly continuous sensor apertures

Adjacent magnetostrictive segments can form bracelet-style arrays conforming to pipe circumference, reducing coverage gaps compared with widely spaced discrete elements. Actual modal purity and noise reduction depend on layout and operating wavelength.

Elevated-temperature performance

FeCo magnetostriction near 800°C

Iron–cobalt alloys can retain magnetostrictive response at temperatures approaching 800°C. This material capability makes FeCo valuable for developing high-temperature guided-wave transducers, provided coils, insulation, magnetic bias and mounting materials are selected accordingly.

The FeCo material capability is not a temperature rating for an assembled sensor. High-temperature plot from the Materials publication to be added when supplied.

About Guided NDE

Guided NDE LLC — Advanced sensor technology since 2008

Guided NDE LLC (GNDE) was founded in 2008 by Dr. Sergey A. Vinogradov. Since its establishment, GNDE has specialized in the design and manufacturing of advanced magnetostrictive transducers (MsT) for guided-wave nondestructive evaluation and structural health monitoring.

The underlying technology has an earlier history: equipment deliveries by Southwest Research Institute (SwRI) began in 1999, while GNDE itself was established in 2008. Since then, GNDE has manufactured and delivered hundreds of thousands of dollars’ worth of specialized magnetostrictive sensors and related equipment to SwRI in support of research and industrial inspection applications.

OCTOBER 2026

Technology licensing and commercial expansion

GNDE entered into a non-exclusive, worldwide patent licensing agreement with SwRI covering specified magnetostrictive sensor technologies. The agreement enables GNDE to manufacture and commercialize products covered by the licensed patents, expanding access to specialized guided-wave sensors for industrial customers.

GNDE’s focus includes sensor manufacturing, equipment delivery and technical support for guided-wave inspection applications.

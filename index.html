Magnetostrictive guided-wave technology

GNDE develops magnetostrictive transducers for generating and receiving ultrasonic guided waves. Magnetic-field orientation controls the type of dynamic strain, while FeCo materials, excitation windings, and bias magnets determine the sensor configuration and efficiency.

Wiedemann and reversed Wiedemann effects

Magnetostrictive guided-wave excitation uses the interaction of static and time-varying magnetic fields to produce alternating shear strain. In the conventional Wiedemann effect, a static circumferential bias field interacts with a time-varying axial field. The reversed Wiedemann effect exchanges these orientations: an axial static bias interacts with a time-varying circumferential field. The resulting shear strain can excite torsional guided waves in cylindrical structures or shear-horizontal guided waves in plates, depending on the configuration.

Reference for the physical principles and sensor configurations: Vinogradov, S., Cobb, A., and Fisher, J. (2018). New Magnetostrictive Transducer Designs for Emerging Application Areas of NDE. Materials, 11(5), 755. See Figure 1 for direct versus reversed field orientations, and Figures 2–3 for the corresponding transducer designs. Read the open-access Materials paper ↗

FeCo sensor construction and magnetic excitation

A magnetostrictive sensor combines a thin iron-cobalt (FeCo) strip, excitation windings that produce a time-varying magnetic field, and permanent magnets providing the static bias. This construction transfers magnetostrictive strain into the inspected structure to launch and receive guided waves.

Coil winding, magnetic bias, and single- or double-sided excitation can be optimized for stronger signals. In the calculated comparison shown below, double-sided excitation produces approximately 3.2–4.6 times greater mid-plane magnetostrictive strain than single-sided excitation at 30, 60, 90, and 120 kHz. The resulting guided-wave amplitude also depends on coupling and inspected geometry.

Source for probe construction and strain comparison: Vinogradov, S., Akimov, N., Cobb, A., and Fisher, J. (2026). Screening of Corrosion in Storage Tank Walls and Bottoms Using an Array of Guided Wave Magnetostrictive Transducers. Sensors, 26(4), 1253, Figure 3a–b. Read paper and Figure 3 ↗

Adaptable form factor. FeCo strips and compact windings allow low-profile sensors, in some configurations only 2–3 mm in installed height. Flexible bracelet arrays conform to pipe circumferences and offer nearly continuous coverage; multiple sensors can also be arranged for larger structures and long-term monitoring.

High-temperature FeCo performance

Experimental studies of Hiperco 50 HS FeCo strips demonstrated guided-wave transduction at temperatures up to 800°C, with signal-amplitude variation on the order of 6 dB. Temperatures below approximately 700°C were identified as more suitable for this material because of recrystallization considerations.

The temperature capability of the FeCo material is not an operating-temperature rating for a complete transducer. Coils, insulation, bias magnets, and mechanical attachment must be qualified separately.

Reference: Vinogradov, S., Cobb, A., and Fisher, J. (2019). Evaluation of FeCo Magnetostrictive Sensors for SHM of Components Operating in Harsh Environmental Conditions. 46th Annual Review of Progress in Quantitative Nondestructive Evaluation, QNDE2019-1234. Read conference paper (PDF) ↗

About Guided NDE LLC

Advanced magnetostrictive sensor technology since 2008

Guided NDE LLC (GNDE) was founded in 2008 by Dr. Sergey A. Vinogradov. Since its establishment, GNDE has specialized in the design and manufacturing of advanced magnetostrictive transducers (MsT) for guided-wave nondestructive evaluation and structural health monitoring.

The underlying technology has an earlier history: equipment deliveries by Southwest Research Institute (SwRI) began in 1999, while GNDE itself was established in 2008. Since then, GNDE has manufactured and delivered hundreds of thousands of dollars’ worth of specialized magnetostrictive sensors and related equipment to SwRI in support of research and industrial inspection applications.

Technology licensing and commercial expansion

In October 2026, GNDE entered into a non-exclusive, worldwide patent licensing agreement with SwRI covering specified magnetostrictive sensor technologies. The agreement enables GNDE to manufacture and commercialize products covered by the licensed patents, expanding access to specialized guided-wave sensors for industrial customers.

GNDE’s focus includes sensor manufacturing, equipment delivery, and technical support for guided-wave inspection applications.

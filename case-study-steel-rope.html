Steel Rope Inspection

Longitudinal guided-wave magnetostrictive inspection

Steel rope inspection uses longitudinal guided waves. The probe arrangement differs from the guided-wave systems used for pipelines and pipe supports.

Inspection concept

One-time inspection. Large permanent magnets provide the magnetic bias. A ribbon coil wrapped around the steel rope generates longitudinal guided waves and receives echoes from changes along the rope.

Permanent monitoring. A flexible, low-profile magnetostrictive sensor stays on the rope for repeated measurements. Each sensor element uses one coil for the DC bias and a second coil to transmit and receive guided waves. The design is described in U.S. Patent 9,465,011 B2.

Case Study

George Washington Bridge suspender rope inspection

Robert Kumapley and Stewart Sloan of the Port Authority of New York and New Jersey reported magnetostrictive guided-wave testing on 21 in-service suspenders in the late 1990s. Selected ropes were removed for destructive testing. In 2005, the inspection program tested 283 rope legs. Read the Port Authority conference abstract (2008, p. 264).

In a field study on the same bridge, Dyab Khazem and colleagues used 10 kHz longitudinal guided waves to examine suspender ropes from a single position above the sidewalk. They recorded reflections from the socket below the sidewalk and from the region over the main cable band on ropes exceeding 100 m in length. Read the Parsons and Southwest Research Institute field paper (PDF).

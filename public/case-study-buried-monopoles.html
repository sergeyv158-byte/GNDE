Guided-Wave Inspection of Buried Steel Monopoles

Flexible magnetostrictive transducer (MsT) arrays provide guided-wave screening near the ground line, where corrosion may develop in steel lighting and utility poles. A sensor array mounted above ground acquires full matrix capture (FMC) data; total focusing method (TFM) processing helps localize reflections from corrosion-related changes. The following two examples are adapted from an ECNDT 2026 paper.

Case 1: Lighting pole embedded in soil

A four-segment MsT array inspected a 102 mm outside-diameter lighting pole. Discoloration near the soil interface suggested corrosion activity. At 180 kHz, the TFM image shows the region near the soil line with greater spatial localization than the corresponding B-scan. The reflection from the end of the pole remained visible.

Case 2: Lighting pole embedded in concrete

For a pole partially embedded in concrete, the steel–concrete interface creates reflections and additional attenuation. Measurements at 180 kHz showed a prominent interface reflection. At 250 kHz its amplitude was lower, consistent with increased guided-wave energy leakage into concrete, while the pole-end reflection remained detectable. These responses must not automatically be classified as corrosion.

Interpretation

Frequency selection affects the detectability and interpretation of indications. FMC/TFM improves spatial representation, but coherent imaging can distort raw reflectivity values; imaging amplitudes should not be treated as calibrated wall loss without suitable correction and validation.

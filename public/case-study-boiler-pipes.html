Multi-Frequency Guided-Wave Inspection of Boiler Pipes

Magnetostrictive transducers (MsTs) were used to collect guided-wave responses from boiler piping at several frequencies without replacing the coil adaptors between measurements. A set of measurements at 20, 32, 45, 64, and 90 kHz illustrates how frequency affects the indications seen in the pipe.

Inspection configuration

The supplied case-study slide shows boiler pipes with multiple bends, accompanied by distance-based A-scans from two pipes. Measurements were collected across a frequency range of 20–90 kHz.

Results

Pipe 1: The plotted responses identify two welds and a pipe-support location. These reference features allow indications to be compared over the inspected distance at different frequencies.

Pipe 2: The traces show an indication marked “Suspect indication” near 20 feet (approximately 6 m), particularly apparent in the lower-frequency data. The supplied slide does not establish the defect type or its dimensions.

Why multiple frequencies matter

The slide distinguishes higher frequencies (60–90 kHz and 180–250 kHz) for searching for localized pitting corrosion and examining regions beneath pipe supports, while lower frequencies (20–50 kHz) are described as more suitable for generalized corrosion. A single installed MsT permits this frequency comparison without changing coil adaptors.
